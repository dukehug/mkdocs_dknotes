## 1. What is Project Closure?

- Project closure is the ==final phase== in a project lifecycle. 

- It's where you warp up and finalize the administrative tasks, evaluate the success of the journey, debrief the clients or stakeholders, and finally disband the project team. 

**Key Steps in Project Closure**

- **Verify Deliverables:** Check that  all original  scope requirements, quality standards, and tasks are fully finished.
- **Obtain Stakeholder Sign-Off:** Get formal written acceptance from the client or project sponsor.
- **Handle Financial Reconciliation:** Close out vendor contracts, pay outstanding invoices, and settle the final budget.
- **Transfer Ownership:** Hand over the final product or service to the operations team or end-user with necessary training.
- **Conduct a Retrospective:** Hold a [lessons learned](https://plane.so/blog/what-is-project-closure) session with the team to discuss what went right or wrong.
- **Archive Documentation:** Store project files, reports, and data securely for future organizational reference.
- **Release Resources:** Reassign team members to new projects and revoke system access


**Why Proper Closure Matters**

• Prevents scope creep by setting a hard stop so you aren't stuck maintaining the work indefinitely.
• Ensures legal and financial compliance.
• Builds long-term client trust and satisfaction

## 2. Project Closure Report 

A project closure report is the final formal document that summarizes a completed project, evaluates its performance against goals, and secures official sign-off.  


**Key Elements to Include**

- Project Overview - State the project name, manager, team, sponsors, and original objectives.
- Deliverables Review - Confirm that the client received and signed off on all required outputs.
- Financial Summary -  Compare the final actual costs against the original budget and close out vendor invoices.
- Performance Assessment - Evaluate timeline adherence, scope management, and success metrics.
- Lessons Learned - Document what went well, what failed, and best practices for future teams.
- Resource Release - Detail the handover of remaining assets and reassignment of team members.
- Formal Sign-Off - Provide signature blocks for key stakeholders to approve official termination
